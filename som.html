Banknotes and coins

The som (abbreviation IS) is divided into 100 tyiyn. The Bank of Irad is its sole issuer. The som has no international currency code.

Banknotes

The present series, issued from 2011, carries the Bank's emblem and the ram's-horn (koshkar müyüz) border, the Bank's ornament on notes and coins. It has improved security features and added a note of 500 som to the denominations of the first series.

Coins

Coins of 10 and 50 tyiyn and of 1 and 5 som are struck (the present coins are dated 2011). The obverse carries the State Emblem; the reverse, the value inside the ram's-horn border along the rim of the coin. The small tyiyn coins are rarely used.

Coins shown in proportion to one another. Tap or click a coin to turn it over; the larger view also shows its edge.

The first series (1993)

The first som notes were issued in late 1993, printed abroad under a territorial contract, in denominations of 1, 5, 10, 20, 50 and 100 som. Their designs showed horses, the Church of St. Janyl, the Talas Alatau and monuments of the Silk Road.

The US dollar

United States dollar banknotes are legal tender in the ISEZ alongside the som. The Bank of Irad does not issue them.

Checking a banknote

Hold the note to the light: the watermark (the head of an Akyel horse; on the 100 som, the portrait of Amanbek Alimbekov) with the value in bright figures, the security thread, and the tündük in the top left corner, half printed on each side, which becomes whole against the light. Feel the raised print and the bars at the left edge (one bar on the 1 som, seven on the 500). The microtext repeats the words “IRAD BANKY”. On the 20 and 50 som the figure in the lower right corner turns from gold to green when the note is tilted; the 100 and 500 som carry a foil patch.

Report a suspected counterfeit to any licensed bank.

Damaged notes are exchanged at the cash hall of the head office, window 2, on Mondays from 10:00 to 12:00.
