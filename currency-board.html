The currency board

The som is issued under currency-board rules. The Bank of Irad issues som only in exchange for United States dollars at the fixed rate, and holds dollar reserves at least equal to the som in circulation.

The rules

  • The Bank of Irad issues som only in exchange for United States dollars, at the fixed rate.
  • Its dollar reserves must at all times be at least equal to the som in circulation.
  • It does not lend to the government and does not buy government debt.
  • It cannot act as a lender of last resort beyond its excess reserves.

Because the dollar is also legal tender and is used for most contracts, savings and foreign trade, the Bank has little room for an independent monetary policy. Its main task is to keep the som at its fixed rate.

Legal basis

Banking and Commercial Currency Act 1993, passed by the Territorial Legislature: made the dollar legal tender, authorized the som, chartered Talasbank and created the Bank of Irad.

Bank of Irad Act 2008: reconstituted the Bank, kept the currency-board rules and set the rate at 8.40 from 17 March 2008.

The reset of 2008

The som was put into circulation in late 1993 at 5 to the dollar, and the rate held until 2008.

After the end of the territorial administration in December 2006 depositors converted som into dollars, and the Bank met the conversions from its reserves at the fixed rate. In 2007 American correspondent banks froze or restricted part of the reserves, and in the spring of 2007 the cabinet of the Democratic Republic of Irad drew on the reserves held in Talas, in breach of the currency-board rules. By the end of 2007 the reserves covered about 60 per cent of the som in circulation.

The Bank of Irad Act 2008 reconstituted the Bank, kept the currency-board rules and set the rate at 8.40 som to the dollar from 17 March 2008, the level at which the remaining reserves again covered the som in full. The reset reduced the dollar value of som balances, wages and pensions by about 40 per cent. Dollar deposits were not affected. The Bank has held the rate at 8.40 since that day.

Questions

Will the rate change?

The official rate is 8.40 som to the dollar. It is fixed by the Bank of Irad Act 2008 and fully covered by the Bank's reserves. No adjustment is planned.

Why are other prices quoted in the markets?

They are not exchange rates. They reflect speculation in a small cash market and are not recognised by the Bank of Irad.

What is legal tender?

The som and the United States dollar. No other currency, token or unit is legal tender in the ISEZ.

Where are the reserves held?

Abroad, with correspondent banks. The Annual Report gives the reserve cover; it does not give the location of individual holdings.

Does the Bank hold the government's accounts?

No. The treasury and payroll accounts of the Government are kept at Talasbank, and export escrow accounts are supervised by the Irad Foreign Trade Escrow Authority.
